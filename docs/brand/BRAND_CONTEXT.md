# CutLab Studio Brand Context

This document is a product-identity synthesis based on the current codebase, route structure, UI copy, README, legal pages, settings model, public profile flow, and team workflows.

It does not describe implementation details. Where the product does not state something directly, conclusions below are inferred from the actual behavior and language of the app.

## Concise Product Summary

CutLab Studio is a local-first production tracker for video editors. It organizes edit projects, client work, deadlines, revisions, salary-batch counting, reports, shared team activity, resources, and public-facing profile pages in one editor-specific workspace.

## What The Product Is

CutLab Studio is a workflow and operations workspace for editing work rather than a generic task manager.

It combines:

- A solo production tracker
- An optional account-backed sync layer
- A small-team collaboration workspace
- A lightweight client and public profile layer
- A reporting and workflow-configuration surface

## What Problem It Solves

The product solves the mismatch between generic project tools and the real operating rhythm of video editing work.

The app is built around problems visible across the product:

- Editors need to track work as edits, deliveries, revisions, review cycles, and publishing deadlines, not abstract tasks.
- Freelance and salaried editors need one place to see both project status and earnings-related progress.
- Small studios need shared visibility without the overhead of a large agency PM or CRM platform.
- Editors need local control of their data, with optional cloud sync instead of account dependency from day one.
- Client context, asset links, review links, and delivery timelines are often scattered across different tools.

## Who It Is Built For

The product is built for people whose work is structured around planning, editing, reviewing, delivering, and getting paid for video work.

## Primary Target Audience

- Freelance video editors
- Solo editors handling multiple client projects
- Editors doing a mix of freelance, salary, and personal-channel work
- Individual creator-operators managing production themselves

## Secondary Audiences

- Small editing teams and boutique studios
- Team owners managing up to five collaborators
- Reviewers and clients who need visibility into shared projects
- Editors who want a simple public profile or studio profile sourced from real work

## Core Workflows

- Create and track projects from planned work through in-progress, review, and delivery
- Organize work by client, work type, due date, notes, and earnings
- Count salary-tagged completed edits toward batch payouts
- Review work through timeline, calendar, feedback, media, and reports views
- Save resource links for folders, briefs, review pages, and handoff materials
- Reuse project templates for common editing scenarios
- Configure workflow stages, project tags, notifications, theme, density, and studio preferences
- Publish a public editor profile from tracker data
- Run a small team workspace with invites, roles, assignments, comments, chat, notifications, and activity history

## Key User Goals

- Know what needs to be edited, reviewed, delivered, or followed up next
- Reduce deadline misses and revision chaos
- Keep project, client, and delivery information in one place
- Track both output and earnings without separate admin systems
- Run a lightweight studio workflow without adopting a heavyweight PM suite
- Preserve control over data by working locally first
- Present a credible public profile without maintaining a separate portfolio system

## User Pain Points Being Solved

- Generic task apps do not map cleanly to editing workflows
- Deadlines, revisions, client notes, and deliverables are easy to fragment across tools
- Salary edit counting is usually invisible or managed manually
- Freelance earnings and salaried output are often tracked separately
- Small teams need collaboration, but not enterprise complexity
- Editors may want to start immediately without committing to account setup
- Client and public-facing context is often disconnected from the real production record

## Product Philosophy

- Editor-first over general-purpose
- Workflow clarity over feature sprawl
- Local-first by default, cloud only when useful
- Real production objects over abstract productivity language
- Practical visibility over complex automation
- Small-team usefulness over enterprise breadth

- More control over active work
- Less ambiguity about what is due and what is blocked

## Unique Differentiators

- Local-first operation is a core product behavior, not a secondary mode
- The product is explicitly shaped around video editing workflows
- Salary-batch tracking is treated as a first-class workflow
- Clients are derived from real project records rather than a separate CRM layer
- Public profiles are generated from operational tracker data
- Team collaboration is intentionally scoped to small editing teams
- Integrations are handled as saved workflow links, not overstated as deep platform sync

## Main Value Propositions

- One focused workspace for edit planning, delivery tracking, revision awareness, and payout progress
- Useful immediately in local mode without mandatory setup
- Flexible enough for solo editors and small teams
- Structured around how editors already think about work: cuts, reviews, deliverables, clients, and batches
- Simpler and narrower than a large PM suite, but more operationally complete than a notes app or spreadsheet

## Competitive Positioning

CutLab Studio appears positioned between generic task/project tools and heavyweight production-management systems.

It is not trying to be:

- A broad all-industry task manager
- A full CRM
- A file storage platform
- A payroll or finance system
- A large enterprise collaboration suite

It is trying to be:

- A dedicated operations layer for editing work
- A simpler alternative to stitching together spreadsheets, notes, calendar reminders, and generic PM tools
- A more grounded workflow tool for editors than visually impressive but operationally vague creator tools

## Tone Of Voice Implications

The product voice should remain:

- Direct
- Clear
- Specific to production work
- Operational rather than promotional
- Confident without exaggeration
- Helpful without sounding managerial or corporate

The product should avoid:

- Startup hype language
- Vague creativity clichés
- Enterprise jargon
- Overclaiming integrations or automation depth
- Abstract productivity language that ignores editing realities

## Visual Design Implications

The existing UI suggests these brand directions:

- A studio/control-room feel rather than a playful creator-tool feel
- Structured layouts, clear panels, and high information legibility
- Editorial typography mixed with practical UI components
- Dark and light themes that still feel work-oriented
- Accent color used as workflow signal, not decoration
- Visual emphasis on order, progress, status, and deadlines

Designers should preserve:

- A sense of operational clarity
- Strong hierarchy
- Dense but readable information surfaces
- A balance between professional polish and day-to-day utility

Designers should avoid:

- Overly soft lifestyle branding
- Excessively abstract motion or visuals
- Consumer-social aesthetics
- Agency-enterprise sterility

## Why Users Would Choose This Product Over Alternatives

- It matches the way editors actually structure work
- It starts as a local tool instead of forcing SaaS dependence
- It covers both creative operations and output tracking
- It includes salary-batch logic that generic tools do not
- It gives small teams collaboration without requiring a full operations platform
- It creates public profile surfaces from the same data already used to run the work
- It removes the need for a separate lightweight CRM just to keep client names and histories straight

## Route And Surface Meaning

The route structure reinforces the product identity:

- `Dashboard` frames the app as a daily operating console
- `Projects` is the source of truth
- `Clients` treats relationships as downstream of real work, not a separate sales system
- `Timeline` and `Calendar` frame work in delivery terms
- `Media`, `Resources`, and `Feedback` center the handoff and revision realities of post-production
- `Templates` and `Settings` support repeatable workflow setup
- `Reports` emphasizes output visibility over business intelligence complexity
- `Team` makes collaboration a scoped extension, not the product’s only mode
- `Profile` and `Organization` extend the tracker into lightweight professional presentation

## Settings Meaning

The settings model shows what the product believes matters operationally:

- Studio identity
- Public identity
- Workflow stages
- Project taxonomy
- Salary counting rules
- Notification preferences
- Team permissions
- Display preferences
- Workspace-level external links

This suggests a product that treats configuration as workflow shaping, not merely personalization.

## Why The Product Exists

The product exists because editing work carries a specific blend of production tracking, revision management, delivery pressure, client context, and payout awareness that generic tools flatten or ignore.

Its identity is rooted in making that work legible and manageable without forcing editors into a bloated operating system.

## One-Sentence Positioning Statement

CutLab Studio is a local-first production tracker built specifically for video editors who need to manage projects, revisions, deadlines, salary progress, and small-team collaboration in one focused workspace.

## One-Sentence Mission Statement

Help working editors run their projects with clarity from plan to delivery while keeping control of their workflow, data, and professional context.
